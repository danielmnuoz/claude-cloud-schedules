US–Iran War — Latest (May 8)

  • Status: Ceasefire technically in force but extremely fragile. The US-Israel military campaign against Iran began February 28 ("Operation Epic Fury"), ending the offensive phase on May 5. A ceasefire has nominally held since April 8, but skirmishes continue.
  • Hormuz confrontation (May 7): US Navy destroyers transiting the Strait of Hormuz exchanged fire with Iranian forces overnight — each side claims the other shot first. Trump called the incident "just a love tap" and said the ceasefire remains in effect. Iran's government called it a ceasefire violation.
  • Peace deal in progress: Iran is reviewing the latest US peace proposal, with Pakistan acting as intermediary ("in continuous contact day and night"). Secretary of State Rubio confirmed the offensive phase is over; Trump warned of "much higher level" strikes if Iran does not sign quickly.
  • Market implication: A credible deal would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, crash oil prices, ease inflation, and strongly boost global risk assets. A ceasefire collapse would push Brent back above $110–120 and pressure equities and growth forecasts.

US–China: Trade & Summit

  • Trump-Xi summit set for May 14–15 in Beijing — Trump's first visit to China in nearly a decade. Expected deliverables include Chinese purchases of US soybeans and Boeing aircraft and a bilateral "Board of Trade" framework for limited tariff adjustments.
  • Iran likely to dominate the agenda, leaving less room to advance on tariffs, rare-earth export curbs, and Taiwan, according to analysts. The Iran focus may delay tangible trade wins.
  • Under the earlier Busan truce, the US agreed to scale back tariffs while China committed to fentanyl enforcement and resuming soybean purchases; implementation has been uneven.

Europe & Other Diplomatic Developments

  • EU tariff deadline: Trump set July 4 as a deadline for the EU to comply with a proposed trade deal, threatening to raise tariffs on EU cars and trucks to 25% from 15% if the bloc does not comply.
  • Russia sanctions: The EU adopted its 20th sanctions package against Russia on April 23; China retaliated by placing several European defense companies on its export control list.
  • Cuba sanctions: On May 1, Trump signed an Executive Order expanding Cuba-related sanctions, building on a national emergency declaration.
