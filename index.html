Fed / Monetary Policy

  • The FOMC held rates steady at 3.50–3.75% on April 29 in what was almost certainly Jerome Powell's final meeting as Chair. The statement retained an easing bias, but three dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) opposed that language; a fourth (Miran) pushed for an immediate 25 bps cut — an unusually divided committee.
  • Powell exits May 15. He will remain on the board of governors (term through 2028) but vowed not to act as a "shadow Fed chair." Kevin Warsh, Trump's nominee to succeed him, cleared the Senate Banking Committee 13-11 on party lines; the full Senate confirmation vote is expected May 11.
  • The Fed is navigating elevated inflation partly driven by the Strait of Hormuz disruption pushing energy prices to four-year highs. Markets have begun pricing in the possibility of a rate hike by 2027 if the conflict persists.

US–Iran War — Day 62 (Developing)

  • The conflict — launched February 28 with US-Israeli airstrikes targeting Iran's military and government sites — is in a brinkmanship phase centered on access to the Strait of Hormuz. A ceasefire agreed April 7–8 remains fragile; no permanent deal has been reached. The US Senate on April 30 rejected a war powers resolution that would have curtailed executive military authority.
  • The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively blocked, disrupting roughly 20% of global oil and LNG shipments. Brent crude briefly topped $126/barrel this week as stalled talks raised doubts about reopening. Iran called a US port blockade "intolerable" and Trump is reportedly weighing further action.
  • US Central Command reports striking over 11,000 targets in Iran since the conflict began. The fighting has spilled into Lebanon (2026 Lebanon War, 2,000+ killed). Iran has maintained one of the world's longest national internet blackouts for most of 2026. Russia's Putin met with Iran's foreign minister and reaffirmed support for Tehran. Formal talks remain stalled as of May 1.
  • Trajectory: Negotiations are stuck on the blockade precondition. A permanent deal hinges on a staged process returning to pre-war status quo before addressing the Hormuz question — but neither side shows urgency. Resolution would be an immediate oil-price shock to the downside.

Trade & Diplomatic Developments

  • US–China trade truce: Trump and Xi met in Busan, South Korea, agreeing to scale back US tariffs in exchange for China resuming US soybean purchases, pausing rare earth export curbs, and cracking down on fentanyl trafficking — a meaningful de-escalation of the 2025 trade war tension.
  • UK export control tightening: New regulations take effect May 13, requiring exporters to obtain licenses when goods risk being diverted to sanctioned destinations — primarily targeting Russia circumvention routes through third-party countries.
  • The Trump administration expanded OFAC enforcement in March (EO 14390), targeting chemical suppliers in India, Guatemala, and Mexico linked to Sinaloa Cartel fentanyl production.

Tech & Business

  • Tech layoffs accelerate in 2026: ~92,000+ workers laid off industry-wide so far this year (nearly 80,000 in Q1 alone), with ~50% of cuts attributed to AI-driven restructuring. Over 900,000 tech jobs have been eliminated since 2020. Largest single layoff: Oracle, 30,000 employees. The trend is broadly hitting software, fintech, e-commerce, and adjacent sectors.
  • Meta will execute cuts of 8,000 employees on May 20 (about 10% of its workforce), with additional rounds planned for H2 2026. CEO Zuckerberg has framed the restructuring as pivoting entirely to AI. Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts — a first in its 51-year history — to redirect headcount toward AI roles.
  • Amazon deepened its Anthropic bet with an additional $25B investment (on top of $8B already deployed), making it one of the largest single AI bets in corporate history. The capital is directed at rapidly scaling AI infrastructure capacity.
  • Apple Intelligence is being cited by Apple's own management as a key driver behind the iPhone's 22% revenue surge in Q2 — the first concrete large-scale signal that on-device AI is converting to upgrade cycles at scale.
  • The "AI spending paradox" continues: companies cutting thousands of human workers are simultaneously committing hundreds of billions to AI infrastructure, raising debate about whether Big Tech will ultimately regret dismantling institutional knowledge.
