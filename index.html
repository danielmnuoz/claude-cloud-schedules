Iran War & Middle East (Day ~71)

  • The 2026 Iran War began February 28 when US and Israel launched coordinated strikes on Iranian military sites and government leadership, including Supreme Leader Khamenei. The offensive phase (Operation Epic Fury) concluded May 5.
  • Ceasefire negotiations are active, with Pakistan mediating. A proposed 14-point deal would commit Iran to a moratorium on nuclear enrichment in exchange for US sanctions relief, release of frozen funds, and mutual reopening of the Strait of Hormuz — Iran is actively reviewing the proposal as of May 6.
  • Strait of Hormuz shipping is at ~5% of pre-conflict levels, driving a historic surge in global energy prices. A credible deal would collapse oil prices, ease inflation, and strongly boost risk assets globally.
  • The UAE is demanding full Hormuz reopening, Iranian reparations, and curbs on Iran's ballistic missile program — signals that a deal faces real regional complexity beyond the US-Iran bilateral.

US–China Trade & Diplomacy

  • Trump-Xi summit scheduled May 14–15 in Beijing — Trump will be the first US president to visit China in nearly a decade. Expected deliverables: Chinese purchases of Boeing aircraft and US agricultural goods, plus a bilateral "Board of Trade" framework for limited tariff adjustments.
  • The Iran conflict is expected to dominate the agenda, potentially crowding out progress on tariffs, rare earth export curbs, and semiconductors — all of which remain unresolved from the earlier Busan truce.

Other Diplomatic & Trade Developments

  • Trump threatened significantly higher tariffs on EU goods unless Brussels drops its tariffs on US exports to zero, rattling European markets late in the week; a July 4 deadline for an EU-US deal looms.
  • The EU adopted its 20th sanctions package against Russia in late April; China retaliated by placing several European defense companies on its export control list.

Tech & Business

AI-Driven Layoff Wave

  • Meta plans to lay off approximately 20% of its total workforce, with a wave of ~8,000 employees scheduled for May 20 and additional cuts in H2 2026, while committing up to $135B in AI infrastructure spending this year.
  • Microsoft announced major headcount reductions alongside Meta; the pair's combined announcements in late April topped 20,000 job cuts, prompting alarm that an AI-driven labor shift is accelerating across the industry.
  • Cloudflare cut 1,100+ jobs (20% of workforce); BILL is cutting up to 30% of staff; Upwork is reducing headcount by ~25%.
  • Over 92,000 tech workers have been laid off in 2026 so far (~1,000/day); Nikkei Asia attributes 47.9% of Q1 tech layoffs directly to AI and automation.

AI Infrastructure Boom

  • Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta collectively plan to spend over $725 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026 — a 77% YoY increase — focused on data centers, custom chips, GPUs, and AI model development. The capital is directly replacing the headcount being cut.
  • Micron's explosive results (Q2 revenue near-tripling on AI memory demand, Q3 guidance implying 260% YoY growth) confirm the AI chip supply chain buildout is accelerating with no near-term signs of a slowdown.

Notable Company News

  • Apple beat Q2 2026 earnings on "extraordinary" iPhone 17 demand — a sign that AI-integrated consumer hardware is translating into sustained upgrade cycles.
  • Figma surged on strong earnings, signaling resilient demand for AI-augmented design tooling even amid broader tech belt-tightening.
  • South Korea's Kospi (+78% YTD) and Taiwan's market are the world's top performers — both driven by Samsung, SK Hynix, and TSMC riding the global AI chip wave.
