US–Iran War — Latest (May 7)

  • Background: The US-Israel military campaign against Iran began Feb 28, 2026; US Central Command has struck over 11,000 targets since then. A fragile ceasefire has held since April 8 — now approaching 30 days — but skirmishes in the Strait of Hormuz continue.
  • Peace talks advancing: The US and Iran are closing in on a one-page memorandum of understanding (MOU) to formally end the war and launch a 30-day period of detailed nuclear negotiations. Iran's government is reviewing the latest US proposal and is expected to hand its reply to mediators today, May 7. Iran's Foreign Minister called an agreement "just inches away" but criticized "maximalist demands."
  • Nuclear sticking point: The US demands at least a 20-year moratorium on uranium enrichment; Iran proposed 5 years. Reports suggest 12–15 years as the likely landing zone.
  • Strait of Hormuz: Remains effectively closed to commercial shipping despite the ceasefire. Reopening it is a core condition of any deal — its resolution would send oil sharply lower and be a positive shock to global markets.
  • Trump's posture: Has renewed bomb threats even while continuing diplomacy, complicating negotiations; markets are pricing a deal as probable but not certain.

US–China Trade

  • A 90-day tariff truce struck in Geneva talks lowered headline rates, but Trump this week accused China of violating the deal to mutually roll back tariffs and ease curbs on critical mineral exports. A Trump-Xi summit is expected in the coming weeks.
  • China invoked its anti-sanctions law in response to US blacklisting of Chinese refineries buying Iranian oil, warning domestic firms against complying with US sanctions.

Europe / Russia

  • The EU adopted its 20th sanctions package against Russia on April 23, extending measures to Chinese and Hong Kong firms. China responded by placing European defense companies on its export control list and warned of further countermeasures.

Iran Sanctions

  • On May 1, OFAC designated Iranian foreign-currency exchange houses and front companies; State Department also sanctioned a China-based petroleum terminal alleged to be trading Iranian oil.
