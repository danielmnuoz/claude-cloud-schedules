Fed / Monetary Policy

  • The FOMC held rates steady at 3.50–3.75% at its April 29 meeting — unchanged for the third consecutive meeting. The statement retained an easing bias, interpreted by markets as a lean toward eventual cuts rather than hikes.

  • Markets currently price no rate changes for the remainder of 2026 and well into 2027, with the longer-run trajectory dependent on resolution of the Strait of Hormuz crisis and tariff-driven inflation pressures.

US–Iran War — Day 63 (Developing)

  • The conflict — launched February 28 with US-Israeli airstrikes on Iranian military and government sites — is now in its 63rd day. A fragile ceasefire agreed in early April holds, but no permanent deal has been reached. Iran sent an updated peace proposal to Pakistani intermediaries on May 1, causing oil prices to pull back from recent highs.
  • The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed, with vessel traffic at roughly 5% of pre-war levels (~150 ships/month vs. ~3,000). The IEA has called the supply disruption "unprecedented." US Central Command reports striking over 11,000 targets in Iran at a cost of approximately $25 billion to the US military.
  • The US Senate rejected a war powers resolution on April 30 that would have curtailed executive military authority, preserving the administration's operational flexibility. Iran called a US naval blockade "intolerable"; Trump is reportedly weighing additional action.
  • The conflict has spilled into Lebanon (2026 Lebanon War, 2,000+ killed); Iran attacked Gulf Arab states with missiles and drones. Iran's Foreign Minister met Putin in Russia to discuss negotiations. Iran has maintained one of the world's longest national internet blackouts throughout the conflict.
  • Trajectory: Iran's new peace proposal is the most significant diplomatic signal in weeks. A breakthrough would trigger an immediate ~20–30% crash in oil prices; a breakdown risks pushing Brent toward $130–140 and locking in a stagflationary energy shock.

Trade Policy & Diplomatic Developments

  • US threatening 100% tariffs on China if Beijing continues purchasing Iranian oil, escalating economic pressure on Tehran while directly targeting the largest buyer of sanctioned Iranian crude.
  • US–China trade truce: Following a Trump-Xi summit in Busan, South Korea, the US agreed to scale back tariffs in exchange for China resuming US soybean purchases, pausing rare earth export curbs, and cracking down on fentanyl trafficking — a significant de-escalation from peak 2025 trade-war tensions.
  • The USTR's Section 301 forced-labor investigation public hearing is set for May 5, with implications for supply chains across Southeast Asia and South Asia.
  • New UK export control regulations take effect May 13, requiring licenses for goods at risk of diversion to Russia — affecting re-exporters across the EU and Asia.

Tech & Business

  • Tech layoffs at crisis scale: The industry laid off nearly 80,000 workers in Q1 2026 alone, with ~50% of cuts attributed to AI-driven restructuring. Total 2026 layoffs have crossed 100,000; over 900,000 tech jobs have been cut since 2020. Oracle's 30,000-person reduction was the single largest 2026 layoff event.
  • Meta is cutting 8,000 employees (10% of staff) on May 20, framing the restructuring as a pivot to becoming an AI-first company. Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts — reportedly a first in its history — to redirect headcount toward AI development roles.
  • Washington Post / CNBC analysis: The same companies collectively spending hundreds of billions on AI infrastructure are using AI efficiency as justification for headcount reductions, but analysts note AI is only the 5th most commonly cited reason for layoffs — behind market conditions, restructuring, closures, and cost-cutting.
  • Apple Intelligence driving iPhone upgrade cycle: Apple management explicitly credited on-device AI features with powering a 22% surge in iPhone revenue in Q2 FY2026, providing the first large-scale evidence that AI features convert meaningfully to hardware upgrade demand.
  • Amazon's Anthropic investment deepened further in 2026 with total committed capital exceeding $33B, making it one of the largest single AI bets in corporate history, directed at rapidly scaling inference infrastructure.
  • The broader "AI spending paradox" debate intensifies: Big Tech is simultaneously cutting tens of thousands of workers and committing hundreds of billions to AI capex, raising questions about whether the destruction of institutional knowledge is a long-term strategic risk.
