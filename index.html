International Markets & Geopolitics

Global Markets

  • Europe is trading cautiously today as investors await the eurozone's preliminary June CPI. Germany, France, and Italy all printed cooler-than-expected inflation this week, reducing ECB rate pressure — European equities have underperformed US peers all year given weaker domestic demand and Iran war energy exposure.
  • Asia: Japan's final June manufacturing PMI remained in contraction; Australia's PMI also soft. China's private-sector (Caixin) manufacturing PMI showed only modest improvement, reinforcing an uneven domestic recovery despite ongoing policy stimulus.

Iran War — Latest

  • The US–Israel war with Iran ("Operation Epic Fury") began February 28, 2026 with strikes that killed Supreme Leader Khamenei. A formal 60-day ceasefire MOU was signed June 17 by Trump and Iranian President Pezeshkian, with negotiations toward a permanent end now underway. War costs are estimated at $34–42 billion.
  • The Strait of Hormuz situation remains unstable: after both sides agreed to lift dueling blockades, Iran re-closed the strait citing continued Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon, a claim the US military disputed. Oil flows through the strait remain disrupted and closely watched.
  • The 60-day ceasefire clock is running — the next major deadline for permanent deal terms falls mid-August. Polymarket currently shows low odds of a permanent deal before that window closes.

Trade Policy

  • The US's broad 10% Section 122 tariff (covering ~60 countries) expires July 24. The USTR has proposed replacing it with a 12.5% Section 301 (forced labor) tariff on Chinese goods; public comments close July 6 with a formal hearing July 7.
  • Effective tariffs on most Chinese goods remain near 30% — the highest of any trading partner. US–China trade deal negotiations are ongoing, focused on restoring prior Section 301 rates.

Commodities & Currency

  • Oil (WTI): Projected to average ~$59/bbl in 2026 — non-OPEC supply is growing 3× faster than demand, keeping prices soft despite the Hormuz disruption. Any confirmed reopening of the strait is a near-term downside catalyst for oil.
  • Gold: ~$4,030/oz, down 11% in Q2 — its worst quarter in decades. Higher Treasury yields and a reversal of dollar-debasement positioning are weighing on bullion; markets are now pricing in rate hikes rather than cuts.
  • USD: Strengthening on hawkish Fed repricing, pressuring EM currencies and commodities.

Tech & Business

  • AI-driven layoffs hit 185,894 workers across 267 events in 2026 YTD; 56% of events explicitly cite AI or automation as the driver. The pattern: cut headcount in support/QA/legacy engineering, redeploy savings into AI infrastructure capex.
  • Oracle executed the year's largest single cut — 30,000 employees — as it restructures around cloud and AI services. Intuit eliminated 3,000 jobs (17% of workforce); Atlassian cut 1,600 (10%); GitLab cut 350 (14%); Meta cut 8,000 while redeploying 7,000 into AI-focused roles.
  • Hyperscalers (Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet) have collectively committed to $700B+ in AI infrastructure spending in 2026, driving demand for optical interconnects, custom silicon, and data center power solutions.
  • Global shipments of 800G-and-above optical transceivers are projected to nearly triple — from 24M units in 2025 to ~63M in 2026 — as AI cluster scale-out makes copper interconnects impractical for bandwidth, distance, and power reasons.
  • AI software (SaaS) valuations remain under pressure: the iShares software ETF fell ~30% from its Sep 2025 peak on fears that AI can perform core software functions cheaper and faster — creating pockets of potential value in quality names.
