US–Iran War — Day 64 (Developing)

  • Status — fragile ceasefire: A ceasefire is nominally in place but tensions remain acute. An IRGC senior official warned May 3 that renewed conflict is "possible" after Trump rejected Iran's latest peace proposal. Al Jazeera reports Trump is simultaneously reviewing a new 14-point plan and laying groundwork for an extended blockade of Iranian ports.
  • Background: The war launched February 28 when US and Israeli forces struck Iranian military sites and government targets; Iran subsequently closed the Strait of Hormuz. Only ~154 vessels transited the strait in March (vs. a normal ~3,000/month), disrupting ~20% of global oil supply. US Central Command has struck over 11,000 targets at an estimated cost of $25B to the US military.
  • Lebanon front: The 2026 Lebanon War (Hezbollah vs. Israel) continues with 2,000+ killed despite a separately declared ceasefire; both sides continue trading fire. Iran attacked Gulf Arab states with missiles and drones early in the conflict.
  • Diplomatic signal: Iran's latest proposal reportedly seeks to resolve all outstanding issues within 30 days — a more aggressive timeline than previous offers. Pakistani intermediaries are actively facilitating. Trump called the offer unsatisfactory; Iran's IRGC says it remains "fully prepared" to resume hostilities.
  • Trajectory: A credible path to Hormuz reopening would trigger an immediate ~20–30% oil price drop and meaningfully ease global inflation; a breakdown risks oil toward $130–140 and a stagflationary lock-in that will define Warsh's opening months as Fed Chair.

Trade Policy & Diplomatic Developments

  • IEEPA tariff ruling: The Supreme Court ruled February 20 that the President cannot use IEEPA to impose tariffs; the Court of International Trade subsequently ordered refunds of collected duties. The government's appeal deadline falls in early May — the outcome could fundamentally reshape the entire US tariff architecture.
  • US–China partial truce: Following a Trump-Xi summit, the US agreed to scale back tariffs in exchange for China resuming US soybean purchases, pausing rare-earth export curbs, and cooperating on fentanyl — a meaningful de-escalation from 2025 peak tensions, though the administration is threatening 100% tariffs on China if Beijing continues buying Iranian oil.
  • USMCA review: USTR has opened its joint review of the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement with a hard July 1, 2026 deadline; agricultural and auto provisions are the key flashpoints.
  • China-Africa zero tariffs: Beijing eliminated import duties for 53 African nations in 2026, deepening South-South trade ties and providing an alternative supply chain anchor outside Western trade frameworks.
  • USTR Section 301: Investigations targeting countries that fail to ban forced-labor goods are underway; public hearing is May 5, with implications for Southeast Asian and South Asian supply chains.
