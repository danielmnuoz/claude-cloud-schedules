US–Iran War — Day 66 (Developing)

  • Background: The war began February 28, 2026, when US and Israeli forces struck Iranian military sites and killed several senior officials including Supreme Leader Khamenei. A ceasefire was agreed in early April but remains fragile.
  • Latest escalation (May 4): Iran launched four missiles at UAE territory — the first such strike since the ceasefire — with one drone sparking a fire at an oil facility in Fujairah and wounding three Indian nationals. UAE schools have shifted to remote learning this week.
  • Strait of Hormuz standoff: President Trump announced the US Navy will escort commercial vessels through the strait; a senior Iranian official warned any US presence in the strait constitutes a ceasefire violation. The US sank seven small Iranian boats in a prior skirmish. One South Korean vessel was struck by an explosion near the strait.
  • Diplomacy stalled: Iran's latest proposal was deemed unsatisfactory by Trump; negotiations are described as deadlocked. Trump said the US is having "very positive discussions" on May 3, but Iran's IRGC warned the conflict "may resume" and said it is "fully prepared."
  • Lebanon front: Israel and Hezbollah continue trading fire despite a separately declared ceasefire; Lebanese authorities report over 2,600 killed and more than 1 million displaced in 2026.
  • Trajectory: Ceasefire collapse risk is elevated. A confirmed US–Iran naval confrontation would likely push Brent above $120/bbl. Any credible Hormuz reopening agreement would be an immediate and significant positive shock to global markets and inflation.
