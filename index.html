International Markets & Geopolitics

  • Asia (July 9): Nikkei surged +924 pts to 67,743 on yen weakness and tech optimism; Shanghai +65 pts to 4,036; Hang Seng –169 pts to 24,030 on geopolitical risk.
  • Europe (July 9): DAX edged +41 pts to 24,939; FTSE 100 fell –69 pts to 10,419 as UK energy-import exposure and rate sensitivity weighed.
  • Iran War — latest update: The US launched new airstrikes against Iran after Tehran targeted commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz on July 6–7 (three commercial vessels hit). President Trump declared the June 14 peace MOU "over." The conflict began February 28 with the US–Israel strike that killed Supreme Leader Khamenei; a ceasefire briefly held April 7–June before collapsing. The EU Aviation Safety Agency has barred flights over Iran, Iraq, and Lebanon airspace through August 31.
  • Strait of Hormuz / shipping: Global shipping rerouting away from the Strait is adding cost and delay to energy and goods trade flows, with cascading effects on inflation and supply chains worldwide.
  • Trade policy: The 10% Section 122 tariff expires July 24 (congressional action required to extend or replace). Trump announced Mexico's reciprocal tariff rises to 30% by August 1. The EU–US trade deal entered force July 1. New Section 301 tariffs of 10–12.5% proposed on 59 countries plus the EU following a forced-labor investigation, with a hearing held July 7.
  • Oil: Brent surged +5.2% to $78.02/bbl; WTI settled at $73.52 — both driven by Hormuz escalation and supply disruption fears. Oil prices are the dominant macro driver this week.
  • Gold: Slipped to ~$4,030/oz (lowest since July 2) as the US dollar firmed on safe-haven demand; analysts see $4,000 as a strong floor with central bank buying likely to resume on dips.
  • Currencies: AUD and CAD weakened despite higher commodity prices as dollar safe-haven demand dominated; yen remained weak, supporting Japan's export-heavy equities.

  • 2026 layoff wave deepens: As of July 8, 267 layoff events have cut 185,894 tech workers YTD (~984/day). AI has been the primary stated reason for a record 4th consecutive month; tech accounted for 31% of all US job cuts in H1 2026, up 83% YoY.
  • Microsoft: Cut ~4,800 roles (sales + Xbox) on July 6, adding to the industrywide toll now exceeding 120,000 tech jobs eliminated in 2026.
  • Oracle: Has reduced headcount by 21,000 (–13%) over the past 12 months as it restructures aggressively around cloud and AI workloads.
  • GitLab & Atlassian: GitLab cut 350 workers (14%) to fund AI infrastructure; Atlassian cut 1,600 (10%) to rebalance toward AI and enterprise sales — both are trading human headcount for compute capacity.
  • Capex vs. jobs paradox: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta collectively guide $700B in capex for 2026 — nearly double combined 2025 spend — even as they cut tens of thousands of jobs. Optical interconnect and datacenter infrastructure are the primary beneficiaries of this investment cycle.
  • AAOI order win: Applied Optoelectronics confirmed a $200M+ 1.6T transceiver order from a major long-term hyperscaler customer, consistent with the 400G→800G upgrade cycle. Analyst targets reset to $140–$190 after the July 2 pullback to ~$115.
