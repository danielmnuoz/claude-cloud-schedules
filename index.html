Market Pulse — Daily Briefing

US Markets & Finance

  • Major indices (Sep 29 close, futures up slightly Wed morning): S&P 500 –0.2% to 7,670.84; Dow –0.3% to 51,349.92; Nasdaq –0.1% to 26,797.54. Futures point to a modest rebound Wednesday, with the S&P 500 index touching ~7,684 intraday.
  • Treasury yields near multi-decade highs: The 10-year yield briefly touched 5.253%, its highest since June 2007, and the 30-year hit 5.6%, its highest since 2002 — keeping rate-sensitive equities under pressure.
  • Fed raised rates in September: The FOMC hiked 25 bps to a 3.75%–4.00% target range on September 16 — the first hike of 2026 and the first since 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh cited persistent core PCE (3.3% in July) and oil-driven inflation, and guided toward one more hike before year-end with cuts not expected until 2028.
  • PCE inflation report today (Sep 30): The BEA releases August Personal Consumption Expenditures price data this morning — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Markets are on edge; a hot print could accelerate rate-hike pricing.
  • Notable earnings (Sep 30): Micron (MU), Conagra (CAG), and Levi Strauss (LEVI) all report today. FactSet Research (FDS) traded higher ahead of its Q4 FY2026 results. CarMax (KMX) beat estimates with Q2 FY2027 revenue up 19.5% YoY to $7.9B, EPS $1.16 vs $0.64 a year ago.
  • Q3 earnings season outlook: FactSet forecasts S&P 500 EPS growth of 28.9% for Q3 2026, which would mark the third consecutive quarter above 25%. The consumer remains resilient despite macro headwinds.

International Markets & Geopolitics

  • Global relief rally today: MSCI Asia Pacific Index surged 1.0%, its strongest session in three weeks, with 10 of 11 sectors advancing. European futures also pointed higher as bond yields steadied into month-end. Trading desks caution this looks like a tactical bounce, not a structural reversal.
  • Iran war — month 7: The US–Israel conflict with Iran, which began February 28, 2026, continues with no ceasefire in sight. The Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea remain disrupted, forcing shipping reroutes. New imagery shows intensified construction at Iran's Pickaxe Mountain underground nuclear facility, raising proliferation alarm. Iran's IRGC is now directly appealing to US voters ahead of the November 3 midterms.
  • Hormuz tensions & peace talks: US officials reportedly met Iran war mediators this week; Trump denied offering Tehran sanctions relief. Saudi Arabia remains exposed to regional spillover; Houthis have launched fresh attacks on Saudi territory and are gaining ground near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. UN envoy held emergency talks with Houthi negotiators to avoid further escalation.
  • US–China tariff deal: After the Trump–Xi summit last week, both sides agreed to cut tariffs on $60B of goods under a "30-for-30" framework — $30B of US imports from China (toys, car seats, holiday goods) and $30B of Chinese imports from the US (coal, agriculture). Analysts call it "a safety valve, not a demolition" — a pause rather than an end to the trade war.
  • Oil above $100/bbl, gold near $4,215/oz: Crude fell on Wednesday after the US released another large tranche from strategic reserves and supply improved from West Asia. Brent still holds above $100. Gold rebounded to ~$4,215/oz and silver to ~$61.50/oz as oil dipped; precious metals had been near 2-month lows on Tuesday.
  • Dollar & global rates: Surging Treasury yields are supporting the US dollar broadly; emerging-market currencies under pressure. India–EU steel trade pact took effect, with India permitted to export up to 1.64M metric tons of steel to the EU annually.

Tech & Business

  • Tech layoffs hit 225,000+ in 2026: As of September 28, 225,122 workers across 519 tracked events have been cut — averaging 828 jobs lost per day. AI is cited as the primary driver, accounting for 87,714 cuts through May alone. Oracle leads with 30,000 eliminated under its "2026 Restructuring Plan" (~$2.8B in charges).
  • September layoff wave: The latest round includes cuts at Uber, PayPal, Apple, and others; companies are explicitly redirecting savings into AI capital expenditure. AI cited in the majority of layoff announcements for the third straight month.
  • OpenAI targets $30B round at $1.4T valuation: Bloomberg reports OpenAI is seeking at least $30B in fresh funding, valuing the company at ~$1.4T (pre-money) — up from $852B as recently as March 2026. Annualized revenue has surpassed $40B (+70% since July). CEO Sam Altman has shelved IPO plans for now, calling the timing "ill-advised." SoftBank surged 6%+ in Tokyo on the news.
  • OpenAI's "Dots" AI agent unveiled: Alongside the funding news, OpenAI debuted a new agentic product called Dots, adding to the competitive pressure on workflow-automation software vendors.
  • AI bubble fears persist: Rising borrowing costs and AI-related restructuring are combining to create volatility; some analysts warn of multiple compression in high-multiple AI-infrastructure names if rate hikes continue.
