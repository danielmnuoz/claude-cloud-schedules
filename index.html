US Markets & Finance

  • S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,431 (+0.50%), Nasdaq at 25,889 (+0.31%), Dow at 51,202 (+0.70%). All three indices posted weekly gains on peace-deal optimism and the SpaceX debut.
  • SpaceX (SPCX) completed the largest IPO in history on June 12 — raised ~$75B at $135/share, opened at $150, valuing the company at $1.77 trillion.
  • Fed funds rate remains at 3.50–3.75%. The June 17 FOMC meeting (Wed) is expected to hold rates steady, but markets are watching closely for Chair Kevin Warsh's inaugural press conference and a potential shift from easing bias to neutral/hawkish. Warsh may drop the dot plot or strip forward-guidance language.
  • Inflation at 4.2% and May nonfarm payrolls of +172K (above expectations) support the hawkish hold. Rate-hike odds for the October FOMC fell to 44% from 66% last week on Iran peace-deal optimism.
  • Top-10 S&P 500 stocks now represent nearly 40% of the index's value, all with AI exposure — concentration risk remains elevated.

International Markets & Geopolitics

  • Iran war approaching formal end: On June 14, mediators announced a memorandum of understanding to be signed June 19 in Switzerland, intended to end the conflict within 60 days. The deal reportedly includes lifting Strait of Hormuz blockades, sanctions relief for Iran, and dismantling of Tehran's nuclear program.
  • Oil prices fell to a two-month low after the peace announcement, declining from above $110/bbl earlier this year. The $85 support zone is the key level to watch. Energy cost relief is easing inflation expectations globally.
  • Gold trading around $4,200/oz, down ~25% from the Jan 28 all-time high of $5,589 as safe-haven demand fades with the peace deal.
  • Bank of Japan meets June 15–16 with expectations building for a rate hike to ~1%. USD/JPY hovering near 160, a level that has historically triggered intervention warnings.
  • Europe growth projections decelerated to 2.1% for 2026 amid the lingering energy shock. Asia and emerging markets benefiting from stronger global manufacturing and AI-related capex.

Tech & Business

  • Tech layoffs hit 184K in 2026 (247 events, ~1,115 jobs/day), a 33% increase YoY. AI displacement is the primary driver, with companies cutting roles in support, content, data entry, and basic coding to fund AI infrastructure.
  • Meta laid off 8,000 workers in its "AI-first" restructuring, redirecting ~7,000 roles toward AI. Oracle's 30,000-person cut was the single largest layoff event of 2026.
  • SaaS "SaaSpocalypse 2.0": The iShares Expanded Tech-Software ETF is down ~30% since Sept 2025. ~$1T wiped from software stocks between mid-Jan and mid-Feb. Names like Workday (down 48% YoY), Microsoft (down 15% YTD), and ServiceNow are being flagged as beaten-down quality.
  • Photonics boom continues: Nvidia committed $4B to Lumentum and Coherent Corp in March. Optical interconnect market valued at $21.9B in 2026, projected to hit $40B by 2031. The thesis: moving data between chips is the AI bottleneck.
